Part 2A • IELTS Visual Identification

The 4 IELTS Chart Types Featured in the Fashion Economics Video

Use these exact chart names and opening structures in your IELTS Academic Writing Task 1 introduction.

1. Line Graph (Line Chart)

Shows continuous trends over time (e.g., Apparel CPI and fiber unit prices from 2010 to 2026).

IELTS Opening Formula “The line graph illustrates changes in... over a 16-year period.”

2. Pie / Donut Chart

Compares shares or proportions out of 100% (e.g., 60% cotton vs. 40% synthetic imports).

IELTS Opening Formula “The pie charts compare the proportions of...”

3. Bar Chart

Compares distinct categories or percentage growth rates side by side (e.g., fiber cost rises).

IELTS Opening Formula “The bar chart compares the percentage growth in...”

4. Combined / Multi-Chart

Pairs two visuals together (e.g., pie charts of material composition + line/bar chart of costs).

IELTS Opening Formula “The pie charts compare..., whereas the bar chart tracks...”

Part 3A • Video Grammar → IELTS Rules

Essential IELTS Grammar Rules from the CNBC Apparel Report

Master active vs. passive trend verbs, -ing turning-point clauses, verb tenses, and prepositions.

Critical Rule #1 Active vs. Passive Voice

Never Write “The Figure Is Increased / Decreased”!

Trend verbs (increase, decrease, rise, fall, grow, surge, plummet) are intransitive — numbers change by themselves, so use Active Voice. Use Passive Voice only when people/companies perform an action on a product.

Video Passive vs. Active Trend ✓ “Apparel imported into the US was made from cotton.” (Passive action on product)
✓ “China’s share plummeted / The figure increased and then decreased.” (Active trend)
IELTS Correction Rule ✗ The figure is increased / was decreased by 14%.
✓ The figure increased / decreased by 14%.
High-Band Grammar #2 Participle (-ing) Clauses

Turning Points & Crossovers: -ing Clauses

Link two movements in one complex sentence using after / before + verb-ing or a comma + overtaking. Never put a past-tense verb directly after before / after without a subject.

Band 8.0 Structural Templates • After reaching a substantial peak of 88k in 2020, petrol car sales fell sharply.
• Sales grew considerably before experiencing a dramatic decline.
• Electric cars surged to 68k in 2022, overtaking petrol cars by 16k.
Avoid This Classic Mistake ✗ ...before experienced a dramatic decline.
✓ ...before experiencing a dramatic decline.
Tense Control #3 Past Simple vs. Present Perfect

Choosing the Right Tense for Your Timeframe

In the video, Present Perfect describes trends up to “today”, whereas Past Simple describes finished years. In IELTS, check the final year on the X-axis!

Exact Examples from the Video • Present Perfect (up to now): “Prices have gone up” / “Ralph Lauren’s stock has soared.”
• Past Simple (finished past): “In 2010, nearly 60% of apparel was made from cotton.”
IELTS Exam Rule If the graph ends in a finished past year (e.g., 2014–2024), use Past Simple throughout (grew, surged, decreased). Use Present Perfect only when the chart says “to today / present”.
Preposition Precision #4 to vs. by vs. at

Prepositions That Control Meaning: to, by, at

Mixing up to and by changes the numbers completely in IELTS Task 1:

Three Essential Preposition Patterns • to (new level / endpoint): surged to 68k / plummeted to 11%.
• by (amount of change / gap): rose by 20% / overtaking petrol cars by 32k.
• at (static level / peak / low): stood at 65k / peaked at 92k.
Article Reminder Write by 2024 (no the before years) and by 32k (no a before plural quantities).

Part 3C • Unit Precision Matrix

How to Describe Numbers: figure vs. percentage vs. number vs. price

Always check the Y-axis and subtitle before choosing your noun.

Universal Safe Noun

The Figure For...

Works safely for percentages (%), thousands (65k), prices ($), and index points.

Examples • The figure for cotton stood at 60%.
• The figure increased to 88k before falling.
For % Charts

The Percentage / Proportion Of...

Use when the axis shows %. Measure gaps in percentage points.

Examples • The proportion of synthetic imports rose to 60%.
• ✗ Never write: the percentage of 60%.
For Countable Items (k)

The Number Of + Plural

Use for countable items (cars, bicycles, garments), even when measured in thousands (65k).

Examples • The number of electric cars surged to 68k.
• ✗ Never write: the amount of cars.
Only For Money ($ / CPI)

The Price / Cost Of...

Use only when currency ($, £, €) or unit price / CPI is explicitly shown.

Examples • The unit price of cotton rose by 20%.
• ✗ Never call 65k cars “the price”.

The two pie charts compare the proportions of natural materials (leather and canvas) and synthetic polymers used in global footwear manufacturing in 2012 and 2026, while the bar chart illustrates the cumulative percentage growth in unit costs for both materials over the 14-year period.

Overall, footwear production for global shoppers shifted dramatically from natural-dominated to synthetic-dominated, while raw material costs rose for both categories, with natural leather and canvas prices increasing twice as fast as synthetic polymers.

In 2012, natural materials accounted for 62% of global footwear manufacturing, whereas synthetic polymers made up 38%. By 2026, however, this pattern had completely reversed: the figure for synthetic polymers surged to 62%, overtaking natural materials by 24 percentage points after natural inputs decreased to 38%.

Over the same timeframe, the unit cost of natural leather and canvas grew considerably, climbing by 24% by 2026. In contrast, the figure for synthetic polymers rose gradually, recording a modest overall increase of 12%.
